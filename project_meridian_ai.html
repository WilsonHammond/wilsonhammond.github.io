Project Meridian treats AI governance as an operating system: ownership, evidence, risk tolerance, monitoring, escalation, and lifecycle accountability.

Scenario

AI adoption outran governance.

Meridian Services Group is a fictional 4,800-person enterprise adopting generative and predictive AI across customer service, human resources, security operations, marketing, legal support, and internal knowledge work. The project begins when leadership realizes AI tools are entering production faster than ownership, risk classification, testing, and evidence requirements are being defined.

Governance Model

Every use case receives an owner and a lifecycle.

The lab creates an AI use-case inventory with business purpose, system owner, vendor, data class, affected stakeholders, decision impact, human-oversight level, risk tier, approval status, and monitoring requirements.

Risk Analysis

AI risk is documented in business language.

The risk register covers sensitive-data leakage, confabulation, harmful bias, prompt injection, automation bias, vendor dependency, traceability gaps, model change, intellectual-property exposure, and insufficient transparency.

Human Oversight

High-impact decisions remain accountable to people.

The oversight plan defines when AI may draft, recommend, triage, or summarize and when a qualified human must independently review, approve, override, or stop the system.

Monitoring & Incidents

Approval is the beginning, not the finish line.

The project defines monitoring for quality drift, policy violations, privacy events, harmful outputs, access anomalies, material model changes, and user complaints, plus an AI incident playbook.
