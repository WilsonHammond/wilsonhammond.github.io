Project Thesis

Cyber risk becomes business risk when controls fail where operations depend on them most.

Project ClaimShock analyzes a fictional healthcare dependency scenario inspired by public reporting on the Change Healthcare incident. The lab does not claim internal access or private knowledge. It shows how a Governance, Risk, and Compliance analyst can translate a major cyber disruption into control gaps, evidence requests, remediation priorities, privacy impact, third party risk, continuity planning, and executive reporting.

Core Risk Themes

What the lab examines

Identity control failure, missing multifactor authentication, remote access governance, ransomware response, protected health information exposure, third party dependency risk, business continuity planning, and executive cyber risk reporting.

Deliverables

What the project includes

Risk register, control gap analysis, control crosswalk, evidence request list, Plan of Action and Milestones, RACI matrix, business continuity review, third party risk assessment, executive summary, and interview talk track.

Framework Lens

Control families addressed

National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework, National Institute of Standards and Technology Special Publication 800 53, ISO 27001, Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act Security Rule concepts, and Center for Internet Security Controls.

Analyst Question

The working logic

What control failed, what evidence proves it, what business process was harmed, who owns remediation, what residual risk remains, and how will leadership know the risk has actually been reduced?
