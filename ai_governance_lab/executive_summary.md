# Executive Summary: Project Meridian

## Executive finding
Meridian Services Group can continue adopting Artificial Intelligence, but the enterprise requires one visible governance process that connects business ownership, risk classification, data use, testing, human oversight, third-party accountability, monitoring, incident response, and lifecycle evidence.

The principal risk is unmanaged AI adoption: use cases entering production without complete inventory, ownership, risk-tiering, evidence requirements, or reauthorization triggers.

## Priority decisions
1. Establish the enterprise AI inventory as the system of record.
2. Require risk classification before production deployment.
3. Require elevated testing and independent human decision authority for high-impact use cases.
4. Restrict sensitive data to approved AI services under documented contractual and technical controls.
5. Require model/system documentation and traceable approval evidence.
6. Govern third-party AI changes as material changes when risk could be affected.
7. Monitor quality, privacy, security, complaints, drift, and policy violations according to risk tier.
8. Create an AI incident-response path with authority to pause systems.
9. Require written residual-risk acceptance above tolerance.
10. Retire systems and revoke access when the business need ends.

## Highest risks
- Sensitive-data leakage: 20/25 inherent risk
- Confabulation in relied-upon output: 16/25
- Prompt injection and restricted-content exposure: 16/25
- Harmful bias in applicant screening: 15/25
- Over-reliance and automation bias: 15/25
