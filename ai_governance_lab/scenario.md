# Project Meridian Scenario

## Organization

Meridian Services Group is a fictional U.S.-based enterprise with 4,800 employees, a national customer base, regulated client data, and a large third-party software ecosystem.

## Business problem

AI adoption has accelerated faster than governance. Business units have introduced generative and predictive AI for drafting, summarization, customer service, recruiting support, security analysis, and marketing. Leadership does not have a complete inventory of use cases, consistent risk classification, evidence requirements, approval gates, or monitoring standards.

## Trigger event

A customer-service pilot sends sensitive customer information to an approved external generative AI service, while a separate HR team begins testing AI-assisted applicant ranking without a documented bias evaluation or formal human-oversight standard. At the same time, Security identifies a prompt-injection test that causes an internal retrieval-augmented assistant to reveal restricted policy content.

No single event creates catastrophic harm. Together, they reveal an enterprise governance gap.

## Executive question

How can Meridian continue using AI without allowing speed of adoption to outrun accountability, privacy, security, fairness, transparency, evidence, and human decision authority?

## Analyst objective

Build a practical AI governance operating model that inventories AI use cases, assigns accountable owners, classifies risk before deployment, defines testing and evidence, establishes human oversight, governs third-party providers, monitors approved systems, responds to AI incidents, documents residual risk, and retires systems responsibly.
