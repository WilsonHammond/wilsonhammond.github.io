Incident Scenario

One vendor identity opened an enterprise attack path.

A hospitality vendor account is compromised through credential phishing. The attacker enters the franchise cloud environment, registers a rogue authentication method, creates an inbox rule to conceal alerts, searches sensitive SharePoint locations, and downloads customer, sponsor, payment-reference, and executive information.

Detection

Separate warning signs became one incident.

The breach remained active for 11 days before identity, mailbox, location, and SharePoint activity were correlated. Impossible travel, unusual authentication changes, overnight access, suspicious inbox rules, and high-volume downloads produced the complete operating picture.

Business Impact

Cyber exposure reached customers, sponsors, and game-day operations.

The scenario includes potential exposure of 18,742 premium customer and sponsor records, 2,316 payment-token references, executive travel information, contract documents, and hospitality operations. Estimated incident cost reaches $14.8 million.

Risk Analysis

Critical inherent risk reduced through structured treatment.

The initial risk score is assessed at 20 out of 25, Critical. Recommended controls reduce the target residual risk to 8 out of 25, Moderate through stronger authentication, least privilege, vendor governance, centralized logging, data protection, and continuity planning.

Root Cause

The central failure was governance over third-party access.

Excessive vendor permissions, weak authentication requirements, incomplete access reviews, fragmented audit logging, inadequate data classification, and unclear ownership allowed a temporary business relationship to become a durable security weakness.

Response Strategy

Containment followed by a 120-day remediation program.

The response includes disabling the compromised account, revoking sessions, removing the rogue authentication method, preserving evidence, reviewing SharePoint permissions, strengthening external identity controls, and improving vendor contract requirements.

Framework Alignment

Controls mapped across major governance standards.

Project Sideline aligns corrective actions with NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, NIST Special Publication 800-53 Revision 5, and ISO/IEC 27001:2022. The mapping connects identity management, supplier governance, logging, monitoring, incident response, and recovery.

Project Deliverables

Evidence prepared for both analysts and leadership.

The project includes an executive brief, technical incident report, quantified risk register, risk treatment plan, control mapping, lessons learned, synthetic event log, architecture diagram, and KQL-style detection queries.

Analyst Perspective

Technical evidence becomes a business decision.

The project demonstrates how an analyst can move from authentication logs and suspicious downloads to business impact, executive reporting, remediation ownership, measurable risk reduction, and stronger third-party governance.
